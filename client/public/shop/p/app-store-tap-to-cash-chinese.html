How Tap to Cash works — and why listing copy is a separate job

Teams assume Apple Cash capability in Wallet covers storefront language. Tap to Cash behavior, Apple Cash enrollment, and App Store Connect actually separate proximity send UX from product-page copy:

  • Wallet → Send or Request → Tap to Cash — iOS 18 path to start a proximity Apple Cash send. Both users hold supported iPhones near each other; sender confirms amount and authenticates. Does not generate Chinese listing text.
  • No username or QR — Tap to Cash replaces typing a recipient handle or scanning a QR for in-person peer payments. Gallery frames may show "Hold iPhones together" art — listing copy should name 隔空送现金 or 轻点转现金 when that is what the screenshot depicts.
  • Apple Cash balance required — sender and recipient need Apple Cash set up on supported devices and regions. Listing copy should match whether your app assumes that setup or only documents it for buyers.
  • NameDrop-style proximity UX — same bring-devices-together interaction pattern as NameDrop, but transfers Apple Cash instead of contact cards. Marketing must not reuse NameDrop 名片投送 vocabulary on Tap to Cash money-transfer frames.
  • Pricing and Availability → China — makes the app downloadable on the China App Store. Separate from language rows.
  • App Store tab → Chinese (Simplified) localization — independent row for title, subtitle, keywords, description, promotional text, screenshots, and caption lines. Empty or English here means China buyers see English fallback regardless of Tap to Cash marketing in your app.

Working Tap to Cash on device and a localized China listing are both required when you sell on the China storefront — neither replaces the other.
