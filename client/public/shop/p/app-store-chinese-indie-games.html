Why indie games stall on the China storefront

US and EU game studios often treat China as “already covered” because the binary runs fine and English IAP strings work in StoreKit. The listing layer tells a different story. On the zh-Hans storefront, an English-only product page signals the game is not maintained for local players — even when the build supports Chinese UI inside the app:

  • Genre mismatch in Search — players search 休闲益智, 肉鸽, 塔防, or 放置 — not “casual puzzle roguelike tower defense idle” pasted from your Steam page
  • Currency confusion — description mentions “gems” and “coins” but screenshot overlays and IAP rows still say English product names with no zh-Hans equivalent
  • Gameplay invisible in captions — screenshots show beautiful art with English taglines like “Build · Fight · Survive” that do not explain the loop Chinese players expect in the first three seconds
  • Age-rating tone gap — combat, cartoon violence, or chance-based rewards described in English marketing slang that does not align with how Chinese players read content warnings on store pages
  • Utility-app phrasing — a freelancer or template translated your listing like productivity software: feature bullets, “smart AI”, “efficient workflow” — language that reads wrong for a game

Better zh-Hans listing copy helps players understand your game before they install. It does not replace in-game localization, server-side economy design, or Apple’s content review decisions.

Genre keywords Chinese mobile gamers actually type

Game discovery on the China App Store runs on genre vocabulary that rarely mirrors English Steam tags or Western ASO spreadsheets. Chinese players mix Simplified Chinese genre nouns with English loanwords where that is how people search — but the mix is genre-specific, not random:

  • Puzzle / casual — 休闲, 益智, 解谜; English “match-3” often stays as loanword when that is the search habit
  • Roguelike / roguelite — 肉鸽 (from “roguelike”) is mainstream mobile search vocabulary, not a literal translation of “procedural death labyrinth”
  • Idle / incremental — 放置, 挂机; “idle” alone rarely carries intent without the Chinese genre frame
  • Strategy / tower defense — 策略, 塔防; subtype terms (自走棋 for auto-battler) matter more than generic “strategy game”
  • Narrative / adventure — 剧情, 冒险; “story-rich” marketing English does not map to a single Chinese keyword
  • Multiplayer / co-op — 联机, 多人; specify async vs realtime when your game supports one mode only

IAP, battle passes, and virtual currency wording

Game economies use vocabulary utility apps never need. Your listing, screenshot overlays, and IAP display names must agree on what players are buying — or checkout feels like a different product:

  • Hard vs soft currency — 钻石, 金币, 体力, 券; English “gems” and “coins” need consistent zh-Hans labels across description, overlays, and IAP rows
  • Season / battle pass — 赛季通行证, 战令; duration and renewal scope stated plainly, not marketing slogans
  • Starter packs and bundles — 新手礼包, 限时礼包; scope (one-time, consumable, permanent unlock) visible in IAP description lines
  • Remove-ads and premium tiers — 去广告, 完整版; align with what the paywall actually removes
  • Gacha-adjacent mechanics — if your game has random pulls or loot boxes, listing copy should describe outcomes honestly without euphemism stacks that trigger distrust or review friction
  • Subscription vs one-time — 月卡, 订阅; Chinese players distinguish monthly stipend packs from auto-renewing VIP subscriptions

Age-rating-adjacent copy on game listings

Apple assigns the age rating in Connect — this page is not rating submission advice. But listing copy still shapes buyer expectations and support load on the China storefront. Indie games often under-describe or over-hype content that players and parents notice immediately:

  • Combat and violence — distinguish cartoon / stylized combat (卡通 violence framing) from realistic depictions when your art style supports it
  • Horror and tension — jump-scare games need tone that matches screenshots; “thrilling adventure” that hides horror genre frustrates installs and refunds
  • User-generated content — if chat or custom levels exist, listing should not imply a fully curated safe space when moderation is limited
  • Chance-based rewards — random drops, loot boxes, and pull mechanics deserve plain scope lines in description, not buried in feature bullet twelve
  • Online interaction — PvP, trading, and clan systems should be visible in copy when they affect the experience minors might access

Clear zh-Hans phrasing reduces “this is not what the store page promised” reviews. It does not replace Apple’s rating questionnaire or local regulatory review.

Screenshot captions that sell a gameplay moment

Game screenshots compete on motion and moment — not feature grids. Chinese storefront visitors often decide from the first two overlay lines whether your loop is 放置, 卡牌, 射击, or something else entirely:

  • Name the loop in six words — “合成升级，一键挂机” beats “Smart · Addictive · Fun” with no verb
  • Show a decision, not an adjective — “选择技能搭配，撑过第30波” beats “Epic battles await”
  • Match caption to frame — if the screenshot shows inventory, caption inventory; if it shows boss fight, caption boss fight
  • Align with localized IAP terms — if overlay mentions 钻石, the purchase sheet should not still say “Gems Pack”
  • Avoid subtitle repetition — captions complement title and subtitle; they do not reprint them

Why a utility-app localization template fails for games

Most cheap localization paths assume productivity software: translate the feature list, mirror English sentence order, ship. Games break that model in predictable ways:

  • Feature bullets without a verb — “Cloud save · Achievements · Leaderboards” translated literally reads like a settings menu, not a game pitch
  • Genre terms missing — description sells “unique experience” with no 肉鸽, 塔防, or 卡牌 anchor for Search intent
  • Marketing superlatives — “best”, “ultimate”, “#1” translated into Chinese where players expect concrete loop description
  • Currency words inconsistent — 宝石 in description, 钻石 on screenshots, “Gems” on IAP sheet — three terms for one economy
  • Screenshot captions as adjectives — “精美画面 · 流畅体验 · 趣味玩法” says nothing about what you do for sixty seconds
  • Steam page pasted into App Store — PC marketing paragraphs with keyboard controls and mod support language that mobile players ignore
  • LLM genre hallucination — model adds 二次元 or 开放世界 tags your art style and scope do not support
