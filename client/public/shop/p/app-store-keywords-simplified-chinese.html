The 100-character Keywords field

In App Store Connect, the Keywords field sits under each locale’s localization row. For Simplified Chinese (zh-Hans), it feeds Search indexing on the China storefront. Buyers never see it on your product page — but Apple’s search index does.

  • 100 characters maximum — comma-separated terms, no spaces after commas unless Connect’s live character counter accepts them. When in doubt, trust the counter in Connect, not a blog post.
  • CJK counting is honest — one Han character counts as one character toward the 100. Mixing Chinese terms with English loanwords (PDF, Pomodoro) is fine when Chinese users search those locally; each character still counts.
  • Indexed for Search — terms here can surface your app in China App Store search results. Indexing rules change; read Apple’s docs rather than treating any article as a page-one formula.

Do not waste the 100-character budget

Apple already indexes visible fields. Repeating them in Keywords burns characters you could spend on search variants:

  • Your app name — already indexed from the title field
  • Your subtitle — visible copy; duplicating it in Keywords adds nothing
  • Your category name or primary category nouns already in the title
  • Competitor app names you do not own — risky and usually a poor use of space

Use Keywords for synonyms, problem phrases, common misspellings, and category variants that are not already visible in title or subtitle.

Why en-US keyword dumps fail on the China storefront

The zh-Hans and en-US keyword rows in App Store Connect are independent. Copying your US row into China is usually wrong. Common mistakes:

  • English spacing habits — “habit tracker, pomodoro timer” — when Chinese users search in Han characters or mixed loanwords
  • US problem phrases that do not match how Chinese users describe the same job
  • Generic ASO filler — “best,” “free,” “app,” “pro” — that US guides recommend but CN Search treats as noise
  • Trending English terms your zh-Hans screenshots and UI do not support

Write terms Chinese users actually type: category nouns (习惯, 记账, 扫描), problem phrases (离线, 无广告, 番茄钟), common misspellings, and English loanwords they search locally (PDF, Pomodoro, VPN) when relevant to your app.

Subtitle — the visible pair to your keyword string

The subtitle sits under your app name on the China product page and in Search results. Apple’s limit is commonly 30 characters for zh-Hans — verify in Connect before you save.

Think of subtitle and Keywords as a pair: subtitle is what buyers see; Keywords is what Search indexes beyond visible copy. Good subtitles add one fact the title does not repeat — audience, workflow, or pricing model. Bad subtitles mirror your keyword string or paste English brochure commas (“智能 · 高效 · 专业”).

One extra fact in subtitle, search variants in Keywords — not the same nouns stacked twice.

zh-Hans vs en-US — separate rows, separate logic

App Store Connect stores keyword strings per locale. Your en-US Keywords row does not automatically apply to China, and China Search behavior differs from US Search. If you ship to both storefronts, plan each keyword string independently — aligned on app meaning, not identical strings. The same app can rank for different queries in each market.
