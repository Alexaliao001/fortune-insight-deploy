One-time-use codes vs custom codes — copy differences

Apple supports multiple offer-code types. zh-Hans strings should match what the buyer expects at entry time:

  • One-time-use codes — single redemption per code; copy should say the code works once and what unlocks: “单次兑换码，解锁 30 天高级版” not a vague “Special offer”
  • Custom codes — reusable campaign codes (e.g. LAUNCH20); listing copy can name the campaign benefit in Chinese without pasting the raw Latin code as the headline
  • Subscription offer codes — codes that attach to a subscription discount; align vocabulary with your IAP tier names so redemption text matches the purchase sheet
  • Non-subscription unlock codes — codes for consumables or non-consumables; description should state one-time vs permanent unlock in plain Chinese

Do not mirror English marketing idioms — “Redeem now”, “Unlock savings”, “Limited code drop” — character-for-character. Chinese buyers expect scope, duration, and what happens after redemption in one or two lines.

Why English-only redemption copy hurts China campaigns

Offer-code campaigns often drive traffic from Chinese social platforms, creator posts, or email — then land buyers on English redemption UI:

  • Creator mismatch — influencer posts in Chinese; buyer taps through and sees “Redeem offer code” with no zh-Hans context
  • Code-entry anxiety — “Enter your code” without Chinese instructions makes buyers fear paste errors or region locks
  • Localized listing, English sheet — description promises a Chinese promo; redemption screen still English at the worst moment
  • Screenshot gallery disconnect — first three screenshots Chinese; code-redemption walkthrough overlays still “Have a code?”
  • Search intent gap — China buyers search 优惠码, 兑换码, or 促销码; English-only keywords miss intent even when codes work

Clearer zh-Hans redemption copy reduces drop-off at code entry. It does not replace code distribution strategy, eligibility rules, in-app paywall UI, or Apple’s review outcomes.

  • Inconsistent vocabulary between listing description, overlays, and subscription offer strings when both promos run concurrently

Machine translation is fine for internal drafts. Finished offer-code strings need idiomatic zh-Hans that states what the code unlocks and how to enter it — while keeping your English product voice. AppLocale rewrites by hand; we do not deliver raw Google Translate or LLM output as finished copy.

What good zh-Hans offer-code copy looks like

Offer-code copy appears for seconds at redemption and on screenshot galleries. Strong Chinese strings lead with the benefit and state entry steps plainly:

  • Overlay: “输入优惠码，解锁 30 天高级功能” — benefit + action in one line
  • Description paragraph: numbered zh-Hans steps — open app → tap redeem → paste code → confirm unlock
  • Offer display name: “新用户专享：首月半价” — not “New User Deal” left English
  • Keywords: include 优惠码 / 兑换码 only when accurate — do not keyword-stuff unrelated terms
  • Consistent tier terms — same “专业版” vocabulary as your IAP rows when codes unlock a subscription tier

zh-Hans and en-US strings can differ in length and structure when the underlying offer is the same — avoid mirroring English comma-heavy promo lines character-for-character.
