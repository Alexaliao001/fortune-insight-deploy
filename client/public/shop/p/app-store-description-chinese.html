Description vs subtitle vs keywords vs update slots

App Store Connect treats listing copy as separate jobs on the zh-Hans localization row. The description is the long body text — up to 4,000 characters — that buyers expand after skimming your title and screenshots. It is indexed for Search alongside title, subtitle, and keywords, but it serves a different job: explain outcomes, walk workflows, and answer objections in readable prose.

  • Subtitle — ~30 visible characters under the app name in Search. Not the description.
  • Keywords — hidden 100-character field. Search terms belong here, not stuffed into description paragraphs.
  • What's New — version-specific release notes tied to each build.
  • Promotional text — up to 170 characters above the description; time-limited news slot.
  • Screenshot overlay captions — short lines on gallery frames, not the listing body.

Why English-only descriptions fail on the China storefront

US and EU indie teams often enable the China storefront before the description is ready. Title and icon look localized; the body still reads like a translated press release. Chinese buyers expect:

  • First two lines that state who the app is for and what outcome they get — before a feature list
  • Plain Simplified Chinese phrasing — not comma-heavy English slogans mirrored word for word
  • Consistent tone with zh-Hans screenshots — not English paragraphs above Chinese gallery art
  • Honest scope — no US download counts, press quotes, or “#1” claims copied without context

Apple may fall back to English when zh-Hans description is empty, but a half-localized page converts worse than a deliberate bilingual strategy. Better description copy reduces product-page bounce — it does not replace in-app UI localization or Apple review decisions.

Character limits and structure

Apple caps the App Store description at 4,000 characters for most locales including zh-Hans — verify in Connect before you save. Structure matters more than filling the box:

  • Above the fold — first ~250 characters appear in the preview snippet before “more.” Lead with use case and primary benefit.
  • Middle — group capabilities under outcomes, not settings-menu module names from your codebase.
  • End — pricing model, privacy stance, or support scope — not keyword stacks or competitor names.

Chinese descriptions often run shorter than English equivalents when prose is tight — you do not need to hit 4,000 characters to convert. Cut duplicate sentences that repeat your subtitle or first screenshot caption.

Keyword stuffing vs readable Simplified Chinese

Some teams paste Search terms into every description paragraph because keywords are hidden elsewhere. On zh-Hans, that reads as spam — and wastes characters Apple may de-emphasize when the same nouns repeat across title, subtitle, keywords, and body.

  • Put synonyms and problem phrases in the keyword field, not repeated ten times in description prose
  • Use natural Chinese sentence flow — not “效率, 专注, 番茄钟, 待办, 习惯” in one line
  • Name features once in plain language: “新增桌面小组件” not three adjectives with no noun
  • Align description opening with what screenshots show — Search brings visitors; description keeps them

Readable zh-Hans copy supports conversion after a Search click. We do not promise any keyword ranks from description edits alone.

Why machine-translated descriptions tank conversion

  • Formal false cognates (“专业级”, “强大”, “极致”) that read as template copy on every competitor page
  • English sentence order preserved — long subordinate clauses Chinese readers skim past
  • Marketing superlatives translated literally without substantiation
  • Feature dumps mirroring your en-US settings menu instead of outcomes Chinese users search for
  • Mixed English product names and Chinese verbs with inconsistent spacing and punctuation

Machine translation is fine for internal drafts. Finished store copy needs idiomatic zh-Hans that matches how Chinese users describe the problem your app solves — while keeping the product voice of your English original. What good zh-Hans descriptions look like

Lead with the outcome, then walk one core workflow. Match tone to a solo indie app — corporate-sounding Chinese reads as outsourced template copy:

  • Paragraph one: who it is for + primary benefit in plain Simplified Chinese
  • Paragraph two: three capabilities grouped by user job, not by your Xcode target names
  • Optional bullets for pricing tiers, privacy, or offline mode — one line each
  • English product names kept where Chinese users expect them; everything else idiomatic

zh-Hans and en-US descriptions can differ structurally when the underlying promise matches — avoid mirroring comma-heavy brochure lines from your English page.
