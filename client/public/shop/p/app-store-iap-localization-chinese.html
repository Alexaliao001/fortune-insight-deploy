IAP copy vs app listing copy

App Store Connect stores in-app purchase strings in a separate localization layer from your app listing. Each IAP product — consumable, non-consumable, auto-renewable subscription, or non-renewing subscription — has its own display name and description per locale. These fields appear on the system purchase sheet, subscription management screens, and (for subscriptions) the App Store subscription detail page — not on your product page gallery or Search snippet.

  • App listing — title, subtitle, keywords, description, promotional text, What's New, screenshot overlays. Different Connect rows.
  • In-app purchases — display name + description per IAP product ID, localized under Features → In-App Purchases → [product] → App Store Localization.
  • In-app UI — paywall screens, pricing labels, and feature gates inside your app binary. Not covered here — you ship those in Xcode.

This page covers IAP display names and descriptions only. For listing fields, see our China storefront listing guide, description guide, or subtitle guide.

Where IAP localization lives in App Store Connect

In App Store Connect, open your app → Features → In-App Purchases (or Subscriptions for auto-renewable groups). Select a product, then add or edit localization rows for each locale — including Simplified Chinese (zh-Hans). Each row has:

  • Display Name — the product title buyers see on the purchase sheet (commonly capped at 30 characters — verify in Connect before you save)
  • Description — one or two lines explaining what the buyer gets (commonly capped at 45 characters — verify in Connect)

Subscription groups share a group-level display name in some views, but individual subscription tiers still carry per-product localization rows. Non-consumables and consumables follow the same display name / description pattern. Empty zh-Hans rows may fall back to English — which is exactly the problem this page addresses.

Why an English-only paywall kills conversion on the China storefront

US and EU indie teams often ship IAP products with English-only localization because StoreKit product IDs and server-side entitlements work fine without zh-Hans strings. The listing looks localized; checkout does not. On the China storefront, that mismatch shows up at the worst moment — when a buyer has already decided to pay:

  • Purchase sheet in English — “Annual Pro”, “Remove Ads”, “Premium Access” with no Chinese context for what unlocks
  • Subscription tier confusion — three English tier names that differ by one adjective (“Basic”, “Plus”, “Pro”) with no zh-Hans differentiation
  • Feature jargon — internal codenames (“Tier 2”, “Bundle A”) pasted as display names because engineers named the StoreKit product first
  • Mismatch with localized listing — Chinese description promises “专业版解锁全部功能” but the purchase sheet still says “Unlock Pro Features”
  • Trust drop at payment — buyers who read Chinese everywhere else hit English at Apple Pay and assume the app is not maintained for China

Better zh-Hans IAP copy reduces paywall abandonment — it does not replace in-app UI localization, pricing strategy, or Apple’s review decisions. This is Connect copy you paste, not code you compile.

Character limits on IAP display name and description

Apple caps IAP strings tighter than listing fields. Count characters in Connect before you save — limits can vary slightly by product type:

  • Display Name — commonly 30 characters for consumables, non-consumables, and subscriptions. This is the product title on the purchase sheet, not your app name.
  • Description — commonly 45 characters. One concrete benefit or scope line — not a mini marketing paragraph.

Chinese IAP names often need fewer characters than English equivalents when phrasing is tight — you do not need to fill every slot. A 12-character zh-Hans display name that states the outcome (“年度专业版”) beats a 30-character English paste. Cut tier adjectives that repeat across products — differentiate by duration or scope instead.

Subscription tiers vs one-time unlocks

Different IAP types need different naming strategies in zh-Hans:

  • Auto-renewable subscriptions — display name should state duration or tier (“月度订阅”, “年度专业版”). Description clarifies renewal and what unlocks.
  • Non-consumables — display name names the permanent unlock (“永久去广告”, “完整功能包”). Description states one-time purchase, no subscription.
  • Consumables — display name states quantity or unit (“100 积分包”, “额外导出次数”). Description clarifies consumable nature.
  • Free trial + paid tier pairs — trial and paid products need distinct zh-Hans names so buyers do not confuse “7-day trial” with “monthly plan” on the sheet.

Align IAP names with what your localized listing promises — if the description mentions “专业版”, the purchase sheet should use the same term, not a different English brand name.

Why machine-translated IAP copy fails at checkout

  • Literal “Pro” → “专业版” on every tier with no differentiation between Basic, Plus, and Pro
  • English duration words preserved (“Monthly”, “Yearly”) instead of Chinese subscription habits (“月度”, “年度”)
  • Marketing superlatives (“Best value”, “Most popular”) translated without context — Chinese buyers expect scope, not slogans
  • Feature lists crammed into the 45-character description field — overflow truncates mid-sentence on the purchase sheet
  • Inconsistent terminology between IAP names and your zh-Hans listing description

Machine translation is fine for internal drafts. Finished IAP copy needs idiomatic zh-Hans that matches how Chinese users describe what they are buying — while keeping the product voice of your English originals. What good zh-Hans IAP copy looks like

At the paywall, buyers decide in seconds. Strong Chinese IAP strings state scope and duration in plain language:

  • Display name: outcome or tier + duration — “年度专业版”, “永久去广告”, “100 次导出包”
  • Description: one line on what unlocks or renews — “解锁全部功能，按月自动续费”
  • Consistent terms across all tiers — if “专业版” means full features, every full-feature tier uses it
  • English product names kept only where Chinese users expect them (e.g. brand names); everything else idiomatic

zh-Hans and en-US IAP strings can differ in length and structure when the underlying promise matches — avoid mirroring English tier adjectives that do not translate.
